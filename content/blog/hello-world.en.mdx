## Why Sports Humanities and Sociology?

From a finance undergraduate degree at South China University of Technology, to a master's in economics at the University of Zurich, to a PhD in Sports Humanities and Sociology at Zhejiang University — this academic path may seem unconventional to outsiders. But for me, every step was a natural progression.

During my five years at the Sports Finance Research Center of Tsinghua PBC School of Finance, I gradually came to realize: **sport is more than an industry**. It is a social issue — one that touches on everyone's health and well-being, on innovations in social governance, and on the implementation of the "Healthy China" national strategy.

## Exercise is Medicine: A Field Worth Digging Into

"Exercise is Medicine" — the deep integration of physical activity and healthcare — is the core focus of my current research.

Traditional healthcare systems prioritize treating existing illnesses, while the unique value of sports and exercise lies in prevention. How can exercise prescriptions be incorporated into public health systems? How do we quantify the health benefits of sports participation? How can incentive mechanisms be designed to encourage more people to be physically active?

These questions are both academic problems and policy challenges. They have no ready-made answers — they can only be explored step by step through research.

## The Unique Value of an Interdisciplinary Background

My training in economics has taught me to analyze problems through a cost-benefit framework, while the perspective of sports humanities and sociology reminds me not to overlook institutions, culture, and individual experience.

This interdisciplinary training helps me, when conducting sports consumption research, to both run regression analyses and understand the cultural logic behind consumption behavior.

I look forward to weaving these two threads even more tightly together in future research.
