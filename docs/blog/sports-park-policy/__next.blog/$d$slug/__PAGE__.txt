3:T8c5,## Policy Background

In 2021, seven government agencies including the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) jointly issued the *Guiding Opinions on Promoting Sports Park Construction*, setting a clear target of building or renovating approximately 1,000 sports parks nationwide by 2025.

This policy did not emerge in a vacuum. As "National Fitness" became a national strategy, the insufficient supply of sports facilities increasingly became a bottleneck. Traditional sports venues often lean toward large-scale, high-end facilities that fail to meet the day-to-day fitness needs of ordinary citizens.

## Findings from the National Survey

During my time at the Sports Finance Research Center at Tsinghua University, I helped organize a nationwide survey of sports parks. The survey covered multiple provinces and cities across eastern, central, and western China. We observed several noteworthy trends:

1. **Integrating Green Space with Sports**: The best sports parks are not necessarily those with the most luxurious facilities, but those that skillfully blend exercise spaces with natural landscapes.
2. **Multi-functional and Compound Needs**: Beyond traditional running tracks, basketball courts, and soccer fields, citizens increasingly demand children's play areas, senior fitness zones, and smart walking trails.
3. **Operating Models Need Urgent Innovation**: Building is easy; sustaining operations is hard. Establishing a viable long-term operating model is the key challenge.

## Policy Recommendations

Based on our survey findings, I authored a policy brief titled *Promoting Sports Park Construction to Meet National Fitness Needs*, which was adopted by the NDRC and published on its official website.

Core recommendations include: strengthening inter-agency coordination (sports, development and reform, housing and construction, natural resources), establishing diversified investment mechanisms, developing tiered construction standards, and improving operational evaluation systems.

## Looking Ahead
